What this course is

Generative AI is already in the practice you are about to join. This course is about using it well: knowing what the tools can do for a lawyer and what they cannot, what the ethics rules require when you use them, and how a firm or a legal aid office should decide to adopt them.

We do that by making decisions, not by reading about them. Which AI platform should a firm approve for confidential client work? What do you do first when AI gets a citation wrong in a document that has already gone out? Where should a firm's AI policy draw its lines? How should a practice bill for work that AI just made five times faster? You will work those questions with the tools open in front of you, commit to an answer with your team, and then defend it against the other teams in the room.

Trying and failing won't harm your grade. Not trying will.

Who it is for

Any 2L or 3L, whatever practice you are headed for: a large firm, a solo practice, public interest, government, in-house. No prerequisites, no coding, and no technical background assumed. If you have never opened an AI tool, you are the student this course was written for. If you use one every day, you will leave knowing when not to.

How class works

I am in the process of redesigning the course for spring around team-based learning. My current thinking is that on the first day I place you on a team, and you stay on that team all semester. The course is organized into seven units, and every unit follows the same shape:

Read
The chapter and one short companion source, before the unit opens.
at home
Readiness check
A short multiple-choice check on the reading. First on your own, then the same questions with your team.
first meeting of the unit
Decide
Your team works a real decision with the AI tools in hand, commits to an answer, and defends it when every team reveals at once.
the rest of the unit

Most of class time goes to the deciding. Lecture is short and aimed at whatever the readiness checks show the room is still unsure about.

My current plan

No group work will take place outside of class. Team work happens in the room, during class time, so no grade of yours depends on scheduling a meeting with teammates or on a teammate doing work outside it.

The seven units

  1. 1
    Foundations
    What generative AI is, how it works in plain terms, and the ways it fails.
  2. 2
    Choosing tools
    The AI tools built for lawyers, and how to evaluate one before a firm trusts it.
  3. 3
    Prompting and retrieval
    Getting useful work out of a model, and grounding it in the right documents.
  4. 4
    Ethics and regulation
    The Model Rules, the ABA's guidance, court standing orders, and the lawyers who were sanctioned.
  5. 5
    The firm
    What AI does to billing, staffing, and the business of practice, and how a firm actually adopts it.
  6. 6
    Access to justice
    Where AI can close the justice gap, and where it must not.
  7. 7
    Transformation
    Online dispute resolution, prediction, and the future of practice.

What I ask of you each week

  • Read before the unit opens. The readiness check is on the reading, and your team is counting on you to have done it.
  • Come to class. The work happens in the room, with your team. It cannot be made up at home.
  • Bring a laptop. You use the AI tools in class, on your own machine.

What is graded

Most of your grade is your own work: the individual readiness checks, short pieces of writing that carry each unit's decision into your own plan, and the capstone. A share of the grade is team work. A small share is your teammates' evaluation of your contribution to the team.

The capstone is an AI integration plan: a plan for how a practice you choose should adopt generative AI, built up across the semester from the decisions the units ask you to make.

There is no final exam.

The textbook

Thomas G. Martin, Generative AI and the Delivery of Legal Services: A Law Student Textbook and Workbook for Understanding and Implementing AI in Law (LawDroid Press). It is an online textbook, free to law students. The author asks that you register at genailawtextbook.com, which also subscribes you to his weekly newsletter; the book itself is read at books.lawdroidmanifesto.com. It was written for law students, assumes no technical background, and comes with mini-lecture videos, podcasts, and a chatbot tutor. There is nothing to buy.

How it differs from LAW 5622

LAW 5622 · Law Practice Technology

Builds the workflows a small firm runs on, in Microsoft 365: intake, calendaring, billing, document automation, e-filing. The tools are the subject.

LAW 5642 · This course

About generative AI: what it can do for a lawyer, what it cannot, what the rules require, and how a practice should adopt it. The judgment is the subject.

They are different courses, and neither is a prerequisite for the other.
